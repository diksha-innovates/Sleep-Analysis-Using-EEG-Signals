SLEEP EEG ANALYSIS PROJECT - INTERVIEW QUESTIONS (50)
======================================================
Organized by topic. Use these to rehearse explaining design decisions,
trade-offs, and the underlying domain knowledge behind this project.


SECTION 1: EEG / PSG / Sleep Science Fundamentals (1-10)
---------------------------------------------------------
1. What is polysomnography (PSG), and which signals does it typically record
   besides EEG?
2. What do the Fpz-Cz and Pz-Oz electrode placements refer to, and why are
   those specific sites used in the Sleep-EDF dataset?
3. What are the five sleep stages used in AASM scoring (Wake, N1, N2, N3,
   REM), and what distinguishes each one in the EEG?
4. Why does the older Rechtschaffen & Kales standard have stages 3 and 4
   while AASM merges them into a single N3 stage?
5. Why is a 30-second epoch the standard scoring window for sleep staging?
6. What EEG frequency bands (delta, theta, alpha, sigma, beta) are
   physiologically associated with which sleep stages?
7. What is a sleep spindle, which frequency band is it found in, and which
   sleep stage is it a hallmark of?
8. Why is EOG (eye movement) recorded alongside EEG for sleep staging, and
   what role does it play in identifying REM sleep?
9. What is "sleep architecture," and how would you describe a typical
   night's progression through stages/cycles?
10. What are the clinical differences between sleep apnea, insomnia,
    narcolepsy, and restless legs syndrome, and why can't frequency-band EEG
    power alone diagnose any of them?


SECTION 2: Signal Processing (11-22)
---------------------------------------------------------
11. Why is a band-pass filter (0.5-40 Hz) applied to raw EEG before
    analysis? What would happen if you skipped it?
12. What's the difference between a FIR and an IIR filter, and why did this
    project use the FIR method in MNE?
13. What does "setting an EEG reference" do, and why does average
    referencing matter for downstream analysis?
14. How do you detect a "flat"/dead EEG channel programmatically, and why
    should such channels be removed before filtering/ICA?
15. What is Independent Component Analysis (ICA), and what artifacts (eye
    blinks, muscle activity, line noise) is it typically used to remove from
    EEG?
16. Why can ICA be computationally expensive on a full overnight recording,
    and how would you mitigate that (e.g. downsampling, limiting
    components)?
17. What is the difference between computing a single FFT over an entire
    signal versus using Welch's method for power spectral density
    estimation?
18. Why is Welch's method generally preferred over a raw FFT for noisy,
    non-stationary biological signals like EEG?
19. How do you compute the "power" within a specific frequency band from a
    power spectral density estimate (e.g. integrating/summing PSD values
    between two frequency cutoffs)?
20. What is spectral leakage, and how do windowing functions in Welch's
    method help reduce it?
21. Why does this project normalize/compute ratios (e.g. delta/total,
    alpha/beta) instead of using raw band power values directly as
    features?
22. What's the risk of computing band power over an entire 24-hour
    recording as one block instead of per 30-second epoch?


SECTION 3: Feature Engineering & Sleep-Stage Classification (23-30)
---------------------------------------------------------
23. How are hypnogram annotations (e.g. "Sleep stage W", "Sleep stage R")
    converted into aligned, fixed-length labeled epochs for supervised
    learning?
24. Why must EEG epochs and their corresponding hypnogram labels be matched
    by timestamp/event rather than assumed to line up by index?
25. What features would you extract per epoch to discriminate between sleep
    stages, beyond simple band power (e.g. spectral edge frequency,
    Hjorth parameters, entropy measures)?
26. Why is the sleep-stage classification problem inherently class
    imbalanced (e.g. much more N2 than N1), and what techniques handle that
    (class weighting, resampling, stratified splits)?
27. Why should a classifier for this task be evaluated with a
    subject-independent split (train on some subjects, test on unseen
    subjects) rather than randomly splitting epochs from the same subject?
28. What's the risk of "leakage" between adjacent epochs within the same
    night when doing a random (non-chronological, non-subject-level) train/
    test split?
29. Why was Random Forest chosen as a baseline classifier here, and what
    other models (SVM, gradient boosting, CNN/LSTM on raw signal) could you
    compare it against?
30. How would you incorporate temporal context (the previous epoch's stage)
    into the classifier, given that sleep stages rarely change epoch-to-
    epoch?


SECTION 4: Classifier Evaluation Metrics (31-38)
---------------------------------------------------------
31. Why is plain accuracy a misleading metric for an imbalanced multi-class
    problem like sleep staging?
32. What does "balanced accuracy" correct for compared to raw accuracy?
33. What is Cohen's Kappa, and why is it commonly reported in sleep-staging
    literature (comparing agreement against human scorers)?
34. What is the Matthews Correlation Coefficient (MCC), and why is it
    considered robust under class imbalance compared to F1-score?
35. What's the difference between macro-averaged and weighted-averaged
    precision/recall/F1, and when would you prefer one over the other?
36. How do you read a confusion matrix to identify which sleep stages are
    most commonly confused with each other (e.g. N1 vs. REM)?
37. Why might N1 typically have the lowest per-class recall/F1 of all five
    stages in sleep-staging models (hint: transitional, short-duration,
    ambiguous EEG signature)?
38. If you had to pick a single metric to report to a non-technical
    stakeholder, which would you choose and why?


SECTION 5: Time-Series Modeling (39-48)
---------------------------------------------------------
39. What does it mean for a time series to be "stationary," and why do
    ARIMA-family models require (or difference the data to achieve) it?
40. What do the (p, d, q) parameters in ARIMA represent?
41. What additional parameters does SARIMA add over ARIMA, and what do they
    capture that plain ARIMA cannot?
42. How would you choose a sensible seasonal period for a SARIMA model
    applied to overnight sleep EEG band power (hint: ~90-minute sleep
    cycles)?
43. What does Auto-ARIMA (pmdarima) do differently from manually specifying
    ARIMA orders, and what criterion (e.g. AIC) does it optimize to pick
    them?
44. How does Holt-Winters Exponential Smoothing differ fundamentally from
    ARIMA in how it models trend and seasonality?
45. What is Prophet designed for, and why might it be a poor or good fit
    for a short, single-night, non-daily-seasonal biological signal like
    this one?
46. Why does an LSTM need a "lookback window," and how does that windowing
    turn a time-series forecasting problem into a supervised learning
    problem?
47. Why must a time-series train/test split be chronological (train on the
    earlier part of the night, test on the later part) rather than a random
    shuffle split?
48. Comparing all six models (ARIMA, SARIMA, Holt-Winters, Auto-ARIMA,
    Prophet, LSTM) by RMSE/MAE/MAPE -- what would it mean if a simple model
    like Holt-Winters outperformed LSTM here, given how little data a
    single night provides?


SECTION 6: Project Architecture & Engineering Decisions (49-50)
---------------------------------------------------------
49. Why was this project refactored from a single notebook into separate
    modules (config.py, data_loader.py, preprocessing.py, features.py,
    classifier.py, timeseries_models.py, evaluation.py, visualization.py,
    main.py)? What specific benefits does that separation give you when
    testing or extending one piece (e.g. swapping the classifier) without
    touching the rest?
50. The original notebook computed its "accuracy" and classification
    metrics on fabricated/hardcoded data instead of real model output. How
    would you catch that kind of issue in a code review, and what practice
    (e.g. asserting data provenance, integration tests, not hardcoding
    "example" arrays next to real pipeline code) would prevent it?
